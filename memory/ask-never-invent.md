**RULE 1**, named as such by Nick on 2026-08-18. Originally given 2026-08-11 after the attention engineering deck. It is the first rule, above everything else in this directory.

**Do not invent stuff.** Not facts, not numbers, not stories, not sections, and not what happened to him. This applies in ordinary conversation, not only when building a deliverable.

When Nick supplies a structure, an outline, or a brief, **build exactly that**. Do not add sections, do not reorder, do not "improve" the spine without asking. And never invent the substance: his personal history, his numbers, his client facts, his offer, his pricing, his terms.

**Why:** two separate proofs.

1. Attention engineering deck (2026-08-11). He gave seven headings. I shipped ten slides, inventing two of them, and then wrote the content of all ten myself, including his 40k loss story, the definitions of his three pillars, and a commercial offer with a 10% holdout and a do-not-renew clause. He never said any of it. Content invented on his behalf reads as fact in a client room and he is the one who has to defend it.
2. Cyprus rental idea (2026-08-18). He said one sentence: looking for a house in Cyprus to rent, left with 100 options, many tabs, decision fatigue, a headache. I invented the whole sequence around it, that the filters had worked, that 100 was the post-filter set, that the tabs came afterwards, then built a diagnosis, a wedge, and a market on top of my own fiction. His answer: "that's not what happened and you are drawing conclusions out of thin air."

**The tell:** output structure wildly out of proportion to input. One sentence in, a product strategy out. When the ratio looks like that, I have filled the gaps myself.

**How to apply:**
- The brief is the spec. Deviations get proposed as a question, not shipped as a draft.
- Every factual slot (a number, a story, a claim about a client, an offer) starts EMPTY and gets filled by him. Placeholder it visibly rather than guessing plausibly.
- A fragment of a story is not a story. Do not reconstruct the order of events, the causes, or what he was thinking. Ask for the sequence.
- Craft is mine: layout, type, hierarchy, motion, code. Substance is his.
- If a section obviously needs something he has not given, ask one specific question rather than filling the gap.
- Ask early and in a batch, before building, not after.
- When caught, do not re-guess in the apology. State what I invented, stop, ask.
