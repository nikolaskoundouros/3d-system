Nick, 2026-10-12, on the hero character shorts: "after you make the stills, i need you to analyze to see if the
reference is the same. for example here i can spot a few differences. his body (waist is leaner), the pants of the
refreence come over the belly button, and finally, the fabric of the pants is different."

**Why:** I showed stills that matched on the things I measured (colour, length) but missed differences he saw at a
glance: body shape, where the garment sits on landmarks (navel), material look. He ends up doing the comparison.

**How to apply:** after every still, before sending: put ours and the reference side by side at the same size and
framing, then go part by part (silhouette widths, where edges sit relative to body landmarks like the navel,
nipples, knees; material: shine, texture, colour; light) and write the differences down. Fix ALL of them in one go before
showing (Nick, same day: "i noticed you keep fixing partially ... if you spot problems, fix them in one go"); only
what truly needs someone else (an editor's sculpt) is named instead. Measure where possible.
Same day he added three checks for characters: muscularity (pecs, waist), joints in their natural range ("the degrees of
freedom of his wrist. our one looks weird"), and above all the fine detail: "the nerves, the pinkish pigment in the
fingernails, the bones that show. thats what makes zack great". Close crops of hands, face, skin side by side, every time.
2026-10-13 dandruff top, Nick: "its not bad but his outfit is horrible, how did this pass your check?" I had spotted it, tried
three times, then parked it as "a separate pass if it matters" and checked only its mean colour. A known difference is never
parked without his decision, and garments are judged on shape, fit, collar and fabric, never on mean colour alone. Related: [[triple-check-before-showing]],
[[references-carry-weight]].

**2026-11-05 miss (king epaulette fringe):** I called fat polished tubes "closer to the ref" from shape alone; Nick: "you need more attention to detail. eg: these look like they are metal". The ref fringe is soft matte cords in the board's tan-gold, curved with the arm, roots hidden. Rule: for every part also ask "what is it MADE of in the ref?" (metal / cloth / thread / skin / wood) and check the material read (gloss, specular streaks, colour family, stiffness, how it hangs) before calling anything closer.
